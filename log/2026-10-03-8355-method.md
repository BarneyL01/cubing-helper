## Checks (js/cube-sim.js under Node; scripts were in the scratchpad)

- Wiki's complete example solve: scramble + all rows → solved (up to the
  whole-cube rotation its d / x2 moves leave).
- Wiki's last-five-corners example 1: solved **only with a final bottom-layer
  turn (D) that the wiki leaves out** — added and marked "(added)". Example 2
  does not reproduce even with other turn directions, so it is not on the
  page.
- Cards (pictures): in each pictured case only the pieces the card talks
  about are out of place — keyhole cards: just the front-right middle edge
  and the bottom front-right corner; long algorithm and its mirror: just the
  one middle edge; plain/flipped swap: that edge pair and the one corner.
- Corner shortcuts: Sassy ×1 = `R U R'` (white facing right), Sassy ×3 =
  `R B U2 B' R'` (up), Sassy ×5 = `U R U' R'` = `F' U' F` (front).
- What the wiki's edge algorithms really do: `R U R' U R U R'` alone moves
  five edge positions, not two; with a trailing `U2` it is a pure swap of
  UF and FR. `F' U' F U' F' U' F` + `U2` is a pure swap of UR and FR with both
  flipped. Rule on the page (yellow sticker of the slot edge faces front →
  plain swap at the front; faces right → flipped swap at the right) was run
  over all 32 arrangements (4 top-layer turns × 4 positions of the top edge
  × 2 types): all solve.
- Keyhole inserts: `R U' R'` (edge at the top front, front sticker = right
  centre colour) and `F' U F` (edge at the top right, right sticker = front
  centre colour) both need the unsolved bottom corner at the front-right and
  touch nothing else in the bottom two layers. `R' U' R' U' R' U R U R` (edge
  above its own right-hand centre) and its mirror don't touch the bottom layer
  at all. The wiki's "F U F'" does something different (it inserts a top-right
  edge into the front-left slot), so `F' U F` is on the page instead.
- Last five corners: the wiki's steps implemented literally and run on random
  legal positions (a few hundred to 2,000 at a time) solved only 23–35% at
  first and 82–84% after the first fixes. Two gaps found and filled:
  (a) a corner already placed at the front-right bottom is pulled back out by
  the next Sassy, so an unsolved bottom corner must be turned under the
  buffer before each round; (b) the two-twisted-corners finish (rotate x' so
  both are in the bottom layer) is impossible when they are at opposite
  corners after lining up the bottom layer, but works whatever the bottom
  layer's alignment if the bottom layer is turned first. Final rule: Sassy ×2
  or ×4 until the front-right corner is solved, turn D' (after x'), Sassy for
  the rest of 6, turn back — 3,000 random positions, all solved, at most 24
  Sassys (mean 15).
- Not checked beyond the example: step 4 part A (the wiki's intuitive "cycle
  through the empty slot"). Says so on the page.
