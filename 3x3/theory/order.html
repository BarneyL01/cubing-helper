Cubing Helper

Order of an algorithm

Do any algorithm again and again and the cube always comes back to solved. The number of times is the algorithm's order: Sassy's is 6.

Our own explanation of standard cube theory. Every order, cycle and picture on this page is worked out by the simulator (js/cube-laws.js); the order found from the cycles was checked against repeating the moves until solved, by log/2026-10-28-theory-check.mjs.

Why it comes back

  1. One go of an algorithm moves pieces round in cycles: A goes to B's place, B to C's, C back to A's.
  2. A cycle of 3 pieces is back after 3 goes; a cycle of 2 after 2.
  3. If a corner cycle also twists (a third of a turn per go), it needs 3 times as many goes; an edge cycle that flips needs twice as many.
  4. The whole cube is back when every cycle is back at the same time: the smallest number that all the cycles' numbers divide into.

Sassy, one go at a time

That is why repeated Sassy is useful in the 8355 last corners: after 3 goes the edges are back and only corners have changed; after 2 every corner is back in its place, just twisted.
