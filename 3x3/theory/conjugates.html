Conjugates

A B A': do a setup A, do an algorithm B, undo the setup. B does its usual job, but on different pieces.

Our own explanation of standard cube theory. What each algorithm changes is worked out by the simulator on the page; the examples are checked by log/2026-10-28-theory-check.mjs.

The idea

  1. A (the setup) moves the pieces you want into the places B works on.
  2. B does its job on those places.
  3. A' (A undone, moves in reverse order, each turned the other way) puts everything A moved back, except that the pieces B changed stay changed.

So a conjugate never needs a new algorithm: it reuses B. Written [A: B]. Every setup move in Old Pochmann, M2 and Orozco is the A of a conjugate.

Making your own

  1. Find an algorithm B that does what you want, on some pieces.
  2. Find moves A that bring your pieces to where B works. Anything else A moves doesn't matter: A' will put it back.
  3. Do A, then B, then A'.

Keep A short, one or two moves, as in the Orozco and Old Pochmann setups.
