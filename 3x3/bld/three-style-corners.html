Cubing Helper

3-Style corners

One commutator for every pair of corner targets, buffer UFR (C): 378 pairs, 8 to 18 moves (12 on average). Each pair is solved in one go, instead of one letter at a time.

From Dylan's 3-Style Learning Sheet (J Perm), its "UFR Comms" and "UFR Types" tabs; not from your notes. The sheet's video on choosing corner commutators: 3-Style Corners. Every commutator was written out and checked in the cube simulator: it moves the buffer's sticker to the 1st target, the 1st target's to the 2nd, the 2nd's back to the buffer, and nothing else. Eleven entries needed a fix; they are marked and listed at the end. No mnemonic words: there are 378 of them.

How to read it

  • Same letters as your other blind pages. The buffer is C (UFR), as for Orozco; J and M are its other stickers.
  • Memo in pairs. For the pair A then B, pick A below and find B in its list.
  • [A : B] means A, B, then A undone: a setup. [A , B] means A, B, A undone, B undone: a commutator. [A : [B , C]] is a setup around a commutator.
  • The type (for example "U-Top / D-Side") is the sheet's own label for where the two targets are.
  • An odd number of corner letters leaves one target without a partner: that needs a parity algorithm at the end. The sheet has a Parity tab; it is not on this page yet.

The same few commutators

Almost every pair is one short core commutator with a setup around it. The setup (in orange) turns the cube so the core can reach the two targets; after the core, the setup is undone. 325 of the 378 pairs use one of eight cores:

  • R' D R, R' D' R, R D R' or R D' R' against a top turn (U, U', U2): 195 pairs. Like Orozco's base.
  • R U R', R U' R', R' U R or R' U' R against a bottom turn (D, D', D2): 130 pairs.

A way in: learn the 41 pairs with no setup first, then those with a one-move setup (58), then two (113). "By commutator" below lists each core with its pairs in that order.
