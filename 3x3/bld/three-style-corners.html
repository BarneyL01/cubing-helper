3-Style corners: Dylan / J Perm

The commutators from Dylan's (J Perm's) 3-Style Learning Sheet: one for every pair of corner targets, buffer UFR (C), 8 to 18 moves (12 on average). Built for speed, so some setups are long; see our table for shorter setups.

From Dylan's 3-Style Learning Sheet (J Perm), its "UFR Comms" and "UFR Types" tabs; not from your notes. The sheet's video on choosing corner commutators: 3-Style Corners. Every commutator was written out and checked in the cube simulator: it moves the buffer's sticker to the 1st target, the 1st target's to the 2nd, the 2nd's back to the buffer, and nothing else. Eleven entries needed a fix; they are marked and listed at the end. No mnemonic words: there are 378 of them.

How it works

Like Orozco: you memo the corners in pairs from the buffer C (UFR), and each pair has one commutator. Every commutator here is a short core with a setup around it (setup in orange):

  1. Setup: moves one or two of the pieces (the buffer and the two targets) into the three spots the core works on. Open any pair to see which pieces go where, with a picture before and after.
  2. Core [A , B] (A, B, A undone, B undone): cycles those three spots, buffer sticker to the 1st spot, 1st to 2nd, 2nd back to the buffer spot.
  3. Undo the setup. Everything else goes back; the two targets are solved.

The sheet also gives each pair a type (for example "U-Top / D-Side"): its own label for where the two targets are; it is shown when a pair is opened. Parity and twisted corners are at the end of the page.

So learn the cores first (each group below), then the pairs with no setup, then one-move setups, and so on. Same letters as your other blind pages; J and M are the buffer's other stickers.
