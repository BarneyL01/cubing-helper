Cubing Helper

Walk rule: which base for any pair

Any two corner letters in one commutator, using only the three helpers (B, P, D) and the setups you know for them. Walk one letter into a helper's place, then set up the other letter that helper's way.

Worked out here (2026-11-10), not from your notes or a published list. Every one of the 378 commutators was checked in the cube simulator (it cycles the buffer and the two letters and moves nothing else), a pair and its reverse always share a setup, and 3,000 whole solves using this table all came out solved. Built by log/2026-11-10-third-helper-set.mjs. Move counts do not include cancellations between the setup and the base.

The rule

  1. Walk one letter into a helper's place. Every letter has one walk: no turn (B, P, D), one turn, or two turns. Walk the letter with the shorter walk. If both walks are the same length, either works; the table on this page keeps the shorter setup.
  2. Set up the other letter the way that helper always does, from where it is after the walk.
  3. Do the base for that position, in the order of the pair: the walked letter counts as the helper. Then undo the setup turns.

All 9 bases

Three bases per helper. "Position first" is the pair position → helper (forwards); "Helper first" is helper → position (backwards): the same base with its halves swapped.

The three helpers

A helper's own setups never move it. One other face reaches its place, and that face does the last turn of every walk to it.

Walks of one turn

Seven letters are one turn from a helper's place. O, E, X and L, T, H are the level 1 quick-test letters from the Orozco and helper P pages.

L and X are also one turn from D's place (L', L2). This page uses one walk per letter: L into P's place, X into B's place.

Walks of two turns

Ten letters need two turns: the first takes the letter onto a one-turn letter's position, the second is that letter's walk.

Exceptions

For five letter pairs the rule gives a setup of four moves. Each has a three-move setup of its own.
