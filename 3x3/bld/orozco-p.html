Orozco with helper P

Your Orozco with P in B's place: the same buffer, the same forwards / backwards rule, but every letter is solved with the step 2 bases, so a whole solve is practice for 3-style.

Worked out here (2026-11-09), not from your notes or a published list. Every commutator was checked in the cube simulator (it cycles the buffer, the letter and P and moves nothing else); the twist was found by a search for the same shape as your Orozco twist; 3,000 whole solves (M2 edges with M2 parity, then these corners, with the parity rule below) all came out solved.   • A pair with P is one commutator, the way a pair with B is in Orozco: "X then P" is just X forwards (P backwards is a skip).
  • The bases are the step 2 ones: the P family [R U R' , D / D' / D2] for L, T, H, and [R' D' R , U2] for A, [R' D' R , U'] for D. See Orozco step 2 for their pictures.

By position: what goes where

Like Orozco's O, H, L: each base has one position, and the setup brings a letter into it. Learn a position at a time: its base, then the letters that go into it.

Every letter

The setup (orange) moves the letter into a base position; the buffer and P never move. Forwards for the 1st letter of a pair, backwards for the 2nd.

P, K and V (the helper's letters)

    Odd number of letters (parity)

    1. Do your edges with M2 as usual, including your M2 parity. That leaves edges A and D swapped, as for Orozco.
    2. Do the corner pairs with helper P. After every pair P is back home.
    3. The last letter X has no partner: do it the plain Orozco way, X forwards with helper B (your Orozco list). That leaves the buffer and B swapped. If X is B do nothing here; N or Q: your Orozco twist rule.
    4. Then the Ra-perm : it swaps the buffer and B back, and edges A and D. Exactly your Orozco parity, so there is nothing new to learn.

    Why not P here: a helper-P letter on its own would leave the buffer and P swapped, and the Ra swaps the buffer with B, not P. Switching to B for the last letter lets the Ra finish it.

    Shortcuts: two letters in one commutator

    With helper P every pair takes two commutators, unless one letter is P. But the same five bases can also solve many pairs directly, without going through P, if the setup may use D turns too (D, L and B: the buffer still never moves, P may move). Then it is one commutator instead of two, like real 3-style.

    Quick test: is L, T or H one of the two letters? Then it is a shortcut (checked for every such pair). L, T and H can play P: one D turn puts them in P's place.

    1. Turn D for L, D' for T or D2 for H: that letter is now where P was.
    2. See where the other letter is now (the D turn moves it too if it is on the bottom).
    3. Carry on exactly as in helper P for that letter: its setup, then its base, forwards if the other letter comes first, backwards if the L, T or H letter comes first.
    4. Undo: the letter's setup, then the D turn.

    A then L: D puts L where P was, A stays, A forwards is [R' D' R , U2], so [D : [R' D' R , U2]].
    E then L: D, then E's setup L' into H, E forwards [D2 , R U R'], so [D L' : [D2 , R U R']]; L then E is the same with [R U R' , D2].

    This covers 45 letter pairs (90 both ways; a few need a third setup move, so they are not all in the list below), 21% of the pairs in a solve, and one comes up in 63% of solves. The other shortcuts below start with an L or B turn that brings a letter onto L, T or H first.
