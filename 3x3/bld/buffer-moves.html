Buffer moves: shorter hard-letter pairs

Your 9 bases (Orozco, helper P, helper D), nothing new: only the setup changes. The walk rule keeps the buffer still, so I, V, Q and U are always far from a base. Let the setup move the buffer and most of their pairs get shorter.

Worked out here (2026-11-11), not from your notes or a published list. Every pair is one of your 9 bases (or its halves swapped) with a setup of at most 3 moves: the shortest with R, U, F and D, or with L or B when that is at least one move shorter. Every commutator was checked in the cube simulator (it cycles the buffer and the two letters and moves nothing else), and so was the walk rule's commutator it is compared with. Built by log/2026-11-11-buffer-moves.mjs. Move counts are written out, after cancelling.

The idea

With the walk rule the setup never moves the buffer, so the base always starts at C. Here the setup may move the buffer too: one of your bases then runs with the buffer in another position (sometimes the buffer's own corner, twisted), and undoing the setup brings it home.
