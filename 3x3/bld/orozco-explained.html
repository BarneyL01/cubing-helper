How Orozco works

One commutator, [R D R', U'], taken apart: what each part does, and why only three corners end up moved.

Our own explanation of the Orozco base commutator for the letter O (see the Orozco page). Every picture and the step runner come from the simulator, so they always match the moves. Where each piece is after each part was traced by simulation (log/2026-10-27-orozco-explained.md).

The idea

A commutator is four parts: X, Y, undo X, undo Y. Here:

  • X = R D R' (the insertion): swaps one top-layer corner with a bottom-layer corner.
  • Y = U' (the interchange): turns the top layer, so a different corner sits in that spot.
  • Undo X = R D' R', undo Y = U.

All 8 moves: R D R' U' R D' R' U.

The three corners

Hold white on top, green in front. Only these three corners are coloured in the pictures; everything else is grey.

  • Buffer (UFR): white, green, red
  • Helper (UBR): white, red, blue
  • O (the corner RDB): yellow, red, blue

The faded squares around the cube show the back and left sides, as if in a mirror.

Part by part

Starting from a solved cube, so you can follow where each corner goes.

Why only three corners move

  • X changes only one spot in the top layer: the helper spot. The rest of the top layer is untouched.
  • Y puts a different corner into that spot (the buffer piece).
  • Undo X reverses X for that spot, but the corner it sends down is now the buffer piece, not the one that came up. Everything else X moved goes back.
  • Undo Y turns the top layer back.

So: buffer piece → O's spot, O piece → helper spot, helper piece → buffer spot. Every other piece, edges included, is back where it started. Halfway through (after Y) the cube looks badly broken; that is normal.

Same shape, other letters

  • Change the D turn: [R D' R', U'] brings up a different bottom corner and solves H; [R D2 R', U'] solves L.
  • Every other Orozco letter is one of these three with a setup first (D or L turns) to bring its corner to where the base expects it. See the Orozco page, By base.
  • Done backwards (the 2nd letter of a pair), the same three corners move the other way round.
