3-Style learned the Orozco way: a few bases, and a short setup of D and L turns that never moves the buffer.

Worked out here (2026-11-05), not from your notes or a published list. A search tried every base (a commutator that solves one pair with no setup: R or R' around a top or bottom turn, against one top or bottom turn) with every setup of up to 4 D and L turns, and kept the shortest setup for each pair, preferring setups that move only one target. Pairs with B are your Orozco commutators; pairs with N or Q use the commutators of the 3-Style page. Every one was checked in the cube simulator: it cycles the buffer and the two targets and moves nothing else. Built by log/2026-11-05-3style-bases.mjs.

How it works

  1. A base is a commutator that solves one pair with no setup. For example [R' D' R , U2] moves buffer → A → P. Your Orozco bases are three of them: buffer → O → B, buffer → H → B and buffer → L → B.
  2. Its reverse (the two parts swapped) solves the pair the other way round: buffer → P → A. Like Orozco forwards and backwards, each group below shows both.
  3. Any other pair: find a base whose positions your targets can reach with D and L turns. The setup moves a target into its base position (for example D' moves K into the G position), the base does its cycle, and the setup is undone. The buffer never moves.

Each pair shows what its setup moves, for example "K into G". Open a pair for the steps, pictures and a cube to watch.
