1. From a scramble to cycles

  1. Find the buffer's letters. DFR is the buffer: V (D face), K (F face), P (R face). Trace from V.
  2. Follow the chain. The sticker in the buffer's V spot belongs to a corner: its letter is the first target. Then take the corner sitting on that letter's spot; its letter is the next target. Stop when a target is V, K or P.
  3. Pair the letters in order (1st and 2nd, 3rd and 4th, and so on). Each pair is one 3-cycle with the buffer, done with a base and a setup (sections 3 to 6).
  4. An odd number of letters, or twisted corners, are on the Twists & parity tab.

2. When the cycle closes early

If the chain returns to the buffer early, start a new chain at an unsolved corner. Its first letter, the break letter (underlined), starts it and runs until a letter of that corner comes up again. The pairs are still taken in list order.

3. The three bases

A base is a commutator that cycles three corners: the buffer DFR, UFR and one more bottom corner. All three use the insertion R U R' and differ in the bottom turn (D, D' or D2). Written the other way round, as [D, R U R'], a base cycles the other way.

3D applets

Each applet plays one base on a solved cube. Drag the cube to turn it.

With only [R U R', D] and its reverse, most pairs need three or four setup moves. The three bases need at most three.

4. How a setup turns a base into any pair

A setup S, a base, then S' (written [S: base]) cycles the three pieces S carries onto the base's corners. To do the pair V → X → Y:

  1. Pick a base. Its sticker cycle is in the table above. For [D, R U R']: V → M → U.
  2. Find a setup that carries V, X and Y onto the base's three stickers in cycle order, starting at any of the three. For [D, R U R'], (V, X, Y) may go to (V, M, U), (M, U, V) or (U, V, M).
  3. Do the setup, the base, then undo the setup.

A setup of only U, L and B turns leaves the buffer where it is, so only the two targets move. The lookup's "Onto" line shows the case.

The three examples come from the table: one, two and three setup moves.

5. Working out a pair yourself

  1. Write the pair: the buffer's piece goes to X, and the piece at X goes to Y.
  2. Pick the base from the bottom corner (DFL, DBR or DBL) close to X and Y: D, D' or D2 for DFL, DBR or DBL.
  3. Choose the direction: the base or its reverse, for the order you need.
  4. Find the setup that moves X onto the base's next sticker without disturbing Y. The lookup gives the shortest.
  5. Check that the three pieces sit on the base's corners. If not, try another start (section 4).

This is a way to search, not a rule for the shortest setup. Section 6 lists the shortest for every pair.

7. What to learn first

  1. The 6 direct pairs: each base alone, in both directions.
  2. Pairs with one setup move: learn them one at a time.
  3. Then two setup moves, then three. Section 4's idea does the work by then.
