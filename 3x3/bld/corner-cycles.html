1. From a scramble to cycles

  1. Find the buffer's letters. DFR is the buffer. Its stickers are V (D face), K (F face) and P (R face). Trace from V.
  2. Follow the buffer. The sticker in the buffer's V spot belongs to some corner. That corner's letter is the first target. Look at the corner that sits on that letter's spot: its piece's letter is the next target. Keep going until the chain reaches a letter on the buffer's corner again.
  3. Pair the letters in order: (1st, 2nd), (3rd, 4th), and so on. Each pair is one 3-cycle: the piece in the buffer goes to the first letter, the piece there goes to the second, and the piece there comes back to the buffer. Each pair is done with a base and a setup (sections 3 to 6).
  4. An odd number of letters, or twisted corners, are on the Twists & parity tab.

2. When the cycle closes early

The chain can come back to the buffer before every corner is solved. Then start a new chain at an unsolved corner. The first letter of that corner (the break letter, underlined) starts it, and the chain runs on until it reaches a letter on the same corner again. A break adds letters to the list, and the pairs are taken in list order as before.

Which sticker starts the break is a free choice, one of the three stickers of the unsolved corner. The table gives the total moves for each choice in the worked example below. The rule used here: pick the break letter that makes the total cheapest. Without the table, take the first letter of the corner in letter order (the Practice tab's rule).

3. The three bases

A base is a commutator that cycles three corners. All three bases below use the same insertion, R U R', and differ only in the bottom turn (D, D' or D2). In every base the buffer corner DFR and the corner UFR are two of the three corners; the third is another bottom corner. Written the other way round ([D, R U R']) a base does the opposite cycle.

One base alone could do every pair, but with longer setups: with only [R U R', D] and its reverse, most pairs need three or four setup moves. Three bases give shorter setups: at most three.

4. How a setup turns a base into any pair

A setup S, then a base, then S' (written [S: base]) cycles the three pieces that S carries onto the base's three corners. Everything else S moved is put back by S'. So to do the pair V → X → Y:

  1. Pick a base. Its three stickers are listed in the table above, in cycle order. For [D, R U R'] they are V → M → U.
  2. Find a setup that carries the three stickers V, X and Y onto the base's three stickers in the base's cycle order. You may start the order at any of the three. For [D, R U R'] the setup may carry (V, X, Y) to (V, M, U), to (M, U, V) or to (U, V, M).
  3. Do the setup, the base, then undo the setup. The pieces cycle, and the rest of the cube is restored.

A setup that leaves the buffer corner where it is (U, L and B turns do) carries V onto V and only the two targets move. Many shortest setups also move the buffer. The lookup's "onto" line shows which case a pair is.

5. Working out a pair yourself

  1. Write the pair from your memo: the buffer's piece goes to X, the piece at X goes to Y.
  2. Look at where X and Y are and which bottom corner (DFL, DBR or DBL) is close to them. That bottom corner is the third corner of the base, so it picks the base: D to DFL, D' to DBR, D2 to DBL.
  3. Choose the direction: the base or its reverse, whichever cycles V → ? → ? in the order you need.
  4. Find the setup: move the sticker X onto the base's next sticker with turns that keep the corner where Y is out of the way, then check that Y lands on the one after. A setup of one to three moves exists for every pair. The lookup below gives the shortest one.
  5. Check before you commit: after the setup, the buffer's piece, the piece at X and the piece at Y must sit on the three base corners. If not, try another start of the order (section 4).

This is a way to search, not a rule that always picks the shortest setup. The table in section 6 is the shortest for every pair (found and checked by a program), and you can hide its answer to practise.

7. What to learn first

  1. The 6 direct pairs. Each base on its own, in both directions. No setup.
  2. The pairs with one setup move. Pick one at a time; the setup is a single turn.
  3. Then two setup moves, then three. By then the idea in section 4 does the work.
