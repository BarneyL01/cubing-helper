1. From a scramble to cycles

  1. Find the buffer's letters. UBL is the buffer, as on the Reference tab. Its stickers are A (U face), E (L face) and R (B face). The Speffz letters below show where each sticker is.
  2. Follow the buffer. The sticker in the buffer's A spot belongs to some corner. That corner's letter is the first target. Look at the corner that sits on that letter's spot: its piece's letter is the next target. Keep going until the chain reaches a letter on the buffer's corner again.
  3. Pair the letters in order: (1st, 2nd), (3rd, 4th), and so on. Each pair is one 3-cycle: the piece in the buffer goes to the first letter, the piece there goes to the second, and the piece there comes back to the buffer. Solving the pair is the commutator for its two letters in the table (section 8).
  4. An odd number of letters leaves one letter over: section 4.

2. When the cycle closes early

The chain above can come back to the buffer before every corner is solved. Then start a new chain at an unsolved corner. The first letter of that corner (the break letter, underlined) starts it, and the chain runs on until it reaches a letter on the same corner again. A break adds letters to the list, and the pairs are taken in list order as before.

Which sticker starts the break is a free choice, one of the three stickers of the unsolved corner. The table gives the total moves for each choice in the worked example below. The rule this site uses: pick the break letter that makes the total cheapest. If you do not remember the table, take the first letter of the corner in letter order (the Practice tab's rule).

3. Twisted corners

A corner that is in its own place but twisted is left out of the letters. It is fixed at the end with a twist row. Directions are seen from outside the corner: clockwise means its U or D sticker moves on to the next face clockwise.

  • Each twist row changes exactly two corners: the buffer and one other corner K. The buffer changes the other way, so the total twist stays zero.
  • To fix K twisted clockwise, use the row whose K turns anticlockwise (and so the buffer turns clockwise).
  • Two twisted corners: use one row for each. The buffer's twists cancel out.

4. An odd number of letters

The corner swap from the Reference tab, R U' R' U' R U R' F' R U R' U' R' F R, swaps the buffer's corner UBL with DFR and swaps the edges UL and UB. With an odd number of corner letters, the last letter Z is done like this:

  1. Turn the cube with a D, F or R setup that brings Z's sticker to P (the F-side sticker of DFR). The setup leaves UBL, UL and UB where they are.
  2. Do the swap algorithm.
  3. Undo the setup. Z's sticker and the buffer sticker A have swapped places, so the cube has the last corner home.

The edges UL and UB end swapped by this algorithm. The Edge cycles tab explains what is and is not covered on the edge side.

5. Easiest cycles to learn

Every direct pair is an 8-move commutator: one single turn (the interchange) and a 3-move insertion. The interchange layer holds two of the three corners, so group the pairs by the layer that is turned and learn one group at a time.

  1. U layer first. The buffer UBL is in the U layer, so it is always one of the two corners the U turn swaps. You only have to find where the other two corners are.
  2. D layer next. Two target corners are in the D layer and the D turn swaps them. The buffer is the corner the insertion brings down.
  3. Side layers last (R, L, F, B turns). These have the most shapes to remember.
  4. Then pairs with one setup move (section 7), once the direct ones feel familiar.

Every pair is in the lookup (section 8). This list only suggests an order.

6. Choosing a setup move

Some pairs have no direct commutator. The Commutators tab's five steps build a commutator from one layer that holds two of the three stickers. When no such layer exists, use a setup move: turn one target sticker into a position where the five steps work, then undo the setup.

  1. Try to build the commutator directly (the Commutators tab, "Building one yourself").
  2. If that fails, do one move that brings one target sticker into a spot where step 1 works. Prefer a move that does not touch the buffer. Do the commutator, then undo the move.
  3. If no single move works, try two moves (or three for the hardest pairs).

No simple rule decides which pairs need a setup. These numbers are the whole table, from the generator.

    7. How to use a setup move

    1. Look up the pair. If its row has a setup, the table gives it.
    2. Do the setup. It carries the two target stickers to spots where a direct commutator exists.
    3. Do the commutator. It solves the pair the setup turned yours into.
    4. Undo the setup: reverse the order and invert each turn. R' F' is undone by F R.
