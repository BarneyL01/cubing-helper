Cubing Helper

Orozco with helper D

Your Orozco with D in B's place: the same buffer and the same forwards / backwards rule. It solves every corner on its own, and its three bases are the D with G / P / S family from the By base page.

Worked out here (2026-11-10), not from your notes or a published list. Every commutator was checked in the cube simulator (it cycles the buffer, the letter and D and moves nothing else); 3,000 whole solves (M2 edges with M2 parity, then these corners, with the F / I and parity rules below) all came out solved. Built by log/2026-11-10-orozco-d.mjs. Why D: only three places can be a helper with two faces free for setups (B, P and D), checked for all 21 letters by log/2026-11-10-third-helper-set.mjs.

  • The bases: [R' D R , U'] for G, [R' D' R , U'] for P and [R' D2 R , U'] for S. As written they move buffer → D → position (backwards); with the two halves swapped, buffer → position → D (forwards).
  • The D turn picks the position, as in Orozco: D = G, D' = P, D2 = S.
  • A pair with D is one commutator: "X then D" is X forwards, "D then X" is X backwards.
  • Any pair of letters in one commutator, with all three helpers: Walk rule.

By position: what goes where

Each base has one position, and the setup brings a letter into it. Learn a position at a time: its base, then the letters that go into it.

Every letter

The setup (orange) moves the letter into a base position; the buffer and D never move. Forwards for the 1st letter of a pair, backwards for the 2nd.

D, F and I (the helper's letters)

    Odd number of letters (parity)

    1. Do your edges with M2 as usual, including your M2 parity. That leaves edges A and D swapped, as for Orozco.
    2. Do the corner pairs with helper D. After every pair D is back home.
    3. The last letter X has no partner: do it the plain Orozco way, X forwards with helper B (your Orozco list). That leaves the buffer and B swapped. If X is B do nothing here; N or Q: your Orozco twist rule.
    4. Then the Ra-perm : it swaps the buffer and B back, and edges A and D. Exactly your Orozco parity, so there is nothing new to learn.
