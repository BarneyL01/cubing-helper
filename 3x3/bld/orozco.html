Cubing Helper

Orozco corners

One commutator per letter instead of Old Pochmann's swap. All 18 are the same three commutators with a short setup of D and L turns.

Orozco is Gabriel Alejandro Orozco Casillas's method (2013). Not from your notes. From the sources (cubinghistory.com; Riu's tutorial on note.com, https://note.com/cuber1u/n/n396edec5a495, read in translation): buffer UFR, helper UBR, 1st target of a pair forwards and 2nd backwards, the published corner list, the helper algorithms and their parity. Notes in docs/orozco-research-notes.md.

Ours: the three-base list (every letter checked to do exactly what the published one does), using your twist algorithm for N and Q (the same effect as the published ones), and the Ra-perm for parity after M2 edges. Checked by log/2026-10-25-orozco-corners.mjs: every published algorithm, and 3,000 random scrambles solved by M2 edges then these corners. Speedsolving.com and Orozco's video could not be read.

How it works

  1. Edges first with M2, as now, including your M2 parity algorithm.
  2. Corner memo from C (the top sticker of UFR), in pairs. Cycle breaks as usual.
  3. 1st letter of each pair: that letter's commutator.
  4. 2nd letter of each pair: that letter's commutator backwards (undo it move by move).
  5. B, N, Q (the helper) have no commutator: see the helper's letters.
  6. Odd number of corner letters: the parity fix at the end.

Why a pair works: forwards X sends the buffer piece to X, X's piece to the helper, and the helper's piece to the buffer. Backwards Y sends the buffer piece to the helper, the helper's piece to Y, and Y's piece to the buffer. Together the helper's piece goes out and comes back: the net effect is buffer → X → Y.
