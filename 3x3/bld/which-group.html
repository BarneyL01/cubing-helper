Cubing Helper

Which group?

Which of the four Orozco groups (B, D, F, K) to use for any pair of corner letters, in three levels. Look up one pair on Pair lookup.

Worked out here (2026-11-16 to 2026-11-18), not from your notes or a published list. All 378 commutators were checked in the cube simulator (each cycles the buffer and the two letters and moves nothing else), and 3,000 whole solves using this table all came out solved. The rule in three levels

All 12 bases

Three bases per helper. "Position first" is the pair position → helper (forwards); "Helper first" is helper → position (backwards): the same base with its halves swapped. Helpers F and K are written as you see them after their rotation.

Which half of the base comes first

The rule: if the letter in the helper's place is the 1st letter of the pair, use the helper's "Helper first" base (backwards). If it is the 2nd letter, use "Position first" (forwards). It is the same 1st / 2nd rule as Orozco.

Which half that puts first (the one-turn interchange or the three-move insertion) depends on the helper, because B's and K's bases are written insertion first and D's and F's interchange first:

HelperLetter in the helper's place is 1stIt is 2nd
B, Kinterchange firstinsertion first
D, Finsertion firstinterchange first

Example: A → G. G goes into K's place and G is the 2nd letter, so Position first: y', then [D : [R D R' , U2]], then y. The insertion R D R' comes first and starts with R, not R'. Checked for all 378 pairs by log/2026-11-19-pair-decide-check.mjs.

When the cycle closes (memo)

Tracing from C, the cycle closes when the next sticker is the buffer's own (C, J or M). Don't write that letter. Check the corners in this order and break into the first one that is not solved (in the wrong place, or twisted), on its first letter:

CheckCorner's lettersNote
AA E R
BB N Qhelper B
DD F Ihelpers D and F
GG L U
HH S X
KK P Vhelper K
OO T W
  • A corner you have already traced counts as solved. The helper corners are often solved: skip them.
  • Write the break letter, trace on as usual, and write the letter you come back to that corner on (another of its letters if the corner is twisted). Then carry on pairing letters as normal.
  • It is the same rule every memo on this site uses (Blind memo, Blind drills, Small solves).

Practise it: Blind drills › Cycle breaks (tick “helper corners solved” for the case where you get stuck).
